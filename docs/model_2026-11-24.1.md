# Message model 2026-11-24.1

Status: **shipped on `main`** (merged 24 Nov build, then updated on the night of 23 Nov for the false flags described in "Update after a live phone test" below). This note says what changed, what each change was for, and what has and has not been measured.

## Why it was rebuilt

The shipped model (`2026-11-23.3`) was trained on 1,184 messages by mistake, learned the weights of 25 hand-written signals and very little from the text, and failed in ways a user notices:

- it missed scams worded a little differently from its few training examples (a QR code "to receive" money, "I sent money by mistake, approve my collect request", investment and task-for-money lures, polite Hindi "do not cut the call", "Aadhaar verification expired");
- it over-flagged everyday Hinglish: polite words such as "aapka", "dijiye", "kijiye" had become a scam cue because in the training data Hinglish was mostly scam text;
- any ordinary link added about four points of "scam" on its own, and a plain "complaint received" notice scored 0.98 in an earlier candidate;
- spelled-out, dotted, look-alike or fancy-font letters ("O T P", "O.T.P", "bl0ck", "𝐊𝐘𝐂") dodged every word list, and Indian comma numbers ("10,000", "25,00,000") were split into pieces.

## What changed

| Area | Change | File |
|---|---|---|
| Text pipeline (Python and TypeScript, kept identical) | Unicode NFKC per character; Cyrillic look-alike letters folded to Latin; chandrabindu unified with anusvara; spelled-out letters joined ("O T P" to "otp"); leetspeak turned back into a word only when the result is in a fixed list of scam-relevant words; Indian comma numbers kept whole; Hinglish currency words ("rupaye", "rupiya") masked as amounts; a shadow feature `x:<latin>` so a Devanagari loanword (ओटीपी) and its Latin spelling (otp) share evidence | `ml/textpipe.py`, `apps/web/src/detector/textpipe.ts`, `ml/canon.json` (a copy lives in `apps/web/src/detector`) |
| Signals | 26 signals (one new: `qr_receive_trick`); prefix matches for Hindi word forms (गारंटी*, कॉल मत काट*); wrong-payment wording; legal-threat vocabulary; investment and task lures; "forward the code" | `ml/signals.json`, `apps/web/public/model/signals.json` |
| Data | Scam families and their matching genuine look-alikes, realistic SMS-inbox formats, everyday Hinglish / Hindi / English messages, and English paraphrases (see below) | `data/team/*_v3.csv`, generators in `ml/gen_*_v3.py` |
| Training | Within Hindi and within Hinglish the minority class is raised to the majority's weight (at most x3), so writing in polite Hinglish stops standing in for "scam"; generated sources share at most 20% of the weighted mass; the **red band** ("likely scam") must keep false alarms at or below 3% of the realistic genuine rows (not weighted), and the **amber band** ("suspicious") must keep recall at or above 98% | `ml/train.py` |

Result of the band policy in the first build: suspicious from 0.18, likely scam from 0.54 (shipped `.3`: 0.10 and 0.35). After the update below the cuts are **0.14 and 0.61**. In practice amber is the broad "check before acting" band and red is kept for messages the model is sure about. An app that notifies the user should notify on red and only list amber.

## What the GPU did

- A fine-tuned `multilingual-e5-small` teacher with group-aware cross-fitting audited the corpus labels (76 confident disagreements in 21,479 rows; mostly UK ringtone spam labelled as scam in the UCI part). It also showed that a transformer does not beat the linear model on the development sets (stress set: AUC 0.996 against 0.999), which confirms that the limit is data variety and not model size.
- The paraphrase generator above (training augmentation) and a rewrite set for measuring robustness (`ml/dev/dev_rewrite_v1.csv`, never trained on).

## Update after a live phone test (23 Nov, night)

The Android app was run on a phone against a real inbox. A genuine Jio missed-call alert ("You have a missed call from +91 ... Thankyou, Team Jio") was shown as **likely scam, confidence high**, and many genuine Jio plan-expiry and offer messages were shown as suspicious or likely scam. Causes and fixes:

- **Missed calls.** The `callback_number` signal (the word "call" and a phone number) was worth about +6.6 points on its own and the training data had almost no genuine message with "call" next to a number. The signal now has exceptions for missed-call, "tried calling", "will call you", "call me" wording (English, Hinglish, Hindi), and `telecom_v3.csv` teaches the model that these are normal. A message that says "Please call 98xxxxxxxx immediately" still fires it.
- **Promotions and plan reminders.** Marketing uses the same words as scams (Congratulations, FREE, immediately, expiring, offer) and the training data had almost no marketing from the real companies; `promo_v3.csv` adds it. In Hindi, the threat signal fired on the word "बंद" in "services will be stopped" inside an ordinary plan-validity reminder; the signal now skips messages that talk about the plan's validity ("प्लान की वैधता", "वैधता समाप्त", ...).
- **Inbox not refreshing.** The Android inbox only listened for a live "SMS received" event, which can miss messages while the app is in the background. It now re-reads the inbox every 8 seconds while open and when the app returns to the front, shows each message's time and a "Checked ..." line, and vibrates for a new amber or red one.

Development sets written for this update (`ml/dev/dev_calls_v1.csv`: 8 scam / 12 genuine; `ml/dev/dev_promos_v1.csv`: 6 scam / 12 genuine; never trained on):

| Dev set | before (cuts 0.18 / 0.54) | after (cuts 0.14 / 0.61) |
|---|---|---|
| calls | scams 8 / 8 red; genuine 11 of 12 shown as likely scam | scams 8 / 8 red; genuine 0 red, 2 of 12 amber |
| promos | scams 6 / 6 red; genuine 3 of 12 red, 4 of 12 amber or red | scams 6 / 6 red; genuine 0 red, 3 of 12 amber |

The earlier development sets are equal or better (stress, SMS, evasion), except the LLM rewrites, where 120 of 131 scams are flagged against 123 before (red is 116 in both) and genuine messages are unchanged (0 red, 4 amber of 152). Still shown amber (not red): a few plan-expiry notices ("Vi pack expires today"), "Prime Mobile free", a shop voucher, "Call back to know more", a clinic call. An amber message is "check before acting", not "scam"; the app cannot tell a real partner offer ("Congratulations! FREE 12-month Canva Pro Lite with your Jio recharge") from a scam that copies it without knowing the sender.

**Test split (group-aware; optimistic, see the evaluation report), now against the first build:**

| | first build (cuts 0.18 / 0.54) | now (cuts 0.14 / 0.61) |
|---|---|---|
| test messages | 3,699 | 3,744 (new generated rows added) |
| shown as at least suspicious: scams caught / genuine flagged | 1,423 of 1,439 / 19 of 2,260 | 1,425 of 1,439 / 38 of 2,305 |
| shown as likely scam (red): scams / genuine | 1,332 of 1,439 / 8 | 1,199 of 1,439 / 14 |

Two costs, stated plainly. The amber band flags more genuine messages (1.6% against 0.8%; some of the new test rows are hard promotions). The red band catches fewer scams (83% against 93%); 127 of the scams that left the red band are one templated family in the public `cloveai` data ("Your meter will be removed today due to non-payment. Call 211 immediately."), whose score now sits around 0.5 and is shown **amber, not red**. Overall recall at the app's warning cut is unchanged (99.0%). The frozen evaluation sets were **not scored again** for this update (the scoring budget was spent), so the numbers in the section below describe the first build.

## Frozen evaluation sets (scored once, with the owner's approval)

`python evaluate.py --measure` was run once on the model on this branch (the fourth scoring of the frozen sets in total). These numbers describe the first build of `2026-11-24.1` (1.59 MB, cuts 0.18 / 0.54, 19,448 training rows); the model was updated afterwards (above) and `docs/evaluation.json` still carries the hashes of that first build. Whether to accept that, or to look for the missed clip types first, is the owner's decision.

## Test it on real messages

The honest test is a real inbox. Put some messages in a CSV with columns `text` and `label` (1 scam, 0 genuine; redact numbers and names; do not commit it), then:

```powershell
cd ml
python check_file.py ../data/real/check.csv
```

It lists every genuine message shown as suspicious or likely scam and every missed scam, with the signals and text pieces that moved the score.

## Known limits

- Hindi remains the thinnest language (about 900 corpus rows, all team-written or translated).
- Look-alike links such as `sbl.co.in` for SBI are not caught by the link checker for short brand names.
- A message with an ordinary link and nothing else suspicious can still land in amber.
- The model sees word patterns, not meaning; a good paraphrase can still fool it.
- The model file is 1.5 MB (the shipped `.3` is 0.34 MB), so the offline cache grows by about 1.2 MB.
