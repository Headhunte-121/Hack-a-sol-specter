# Retrain candidate 2026-11-23.4: evaluated, rejected, not shipped

**Decision.** The candidate was scored on the frozen evaluation sets once as a final model, on 23 Nov 2026, against the acceptance rules of the retrain handoff (D5). It fails items 2, 4 and 5, none by a small margin, so the rollback rule applied: the app keeps model `2026-11-23.3`, its `signals.json`, `domains.json` and the parity and golden fixtures, exactly as on `main`. The leak guard, the cleaned data, the build guards and the new training and evaluation code are kept. Nothing was tuned on the evaluation sets: the three allowed scoring sessions were the baseline check, one after hard-negative round 1, and this one.

**Reproduce.** `cd ml; python build_corpus.py; python train.py` writes `ml/artifacts/candidate/model.json` (SHA-256 `8ae68abc...` = `8AE68ABCC2E0054A1947085D4B93AC8C7C5524FC01F2F6156927C3036DA040BE`, 1.45 MB, 49,945 features, cuts 0.31 / 0.46, trained on 17,467 messages, seed 7). `python evaluate.py --model artifacts/candidate/model.json` scores it (the scoring of the frozen sets is refused: the budget log in `ml/artifacts/measurements.json` is used up). `python train.py --ship` would replace the app's model; do not, unless a later evaluation meets the rules.

## What the numbers say

- **The model ranks well; the operating point is the problem.** AUC-ROC is 0.991 on the probe set, 0.999 on blind_v1, 0.989 on the challenge set and 0.972 on the clip texts. Recall on the challenge set is back to 0.887 (the merged model had 0.787) and genuine messages are still flagged rarely (6.0% on the probe set, 0 of 18 OTP messages).
- **No single cut met the probe set's two limits together, in either measured state.** After round 1 (cuts 0.25 / 0.50; data without the second hard-negative batch) the counts were: probe caught 395 / flagged 43 of 381, OTP 0 of 18, clips caught 19 / flagged 1, Hindi clips 12 / 1, evasion 139, blind_v1 74 / 1, challenge at 0.5: recall 0.963, F1 0.969, false-alarm 0.025. Final (cuts 0.31 / 0.46): 377 / 23, 15 of 22 clips, evasion 117. Going from the first to the second moves along the same trade-off (the probe AUC is 0.993 and 0.991), not off it: fewer false flags, fewer scams caught. The limits "at most 30 false flags and at least 386 scams caught" together ask for a better separator than either state.
- **The limits of items 2, 4 and 5 were set from partly flattered baselines.** Model `2026-11-23.3` reached 387 caught / 25 flagged on the probe set and 19 of 22 clips; the handoff notes these are flattered by the leak "by a handful of items each", which explains only a few of the missing catches. Model `2026-11-23.2` reached 141 evasion catches at a suspicious cut of 0.05, with 146 of 381 genuine probe messages flagged (38%). Reaching 141 evasion catches and at most 30 false flags together has not been shown possible for any model here.
- **The validation policy cannot place the cut for independent text.** In the runs I made, recall on the realistic validation slice (team-written rows) stayed at 0.97 or higher for any suspicious cut from 0.05 up to about 0.5, and precision barely moved, because the scores are almost all near 0 or 1. Depending on the data split and on how the slice was built, the policy gave cuts of 0.57 / 0.82, 0.25 / 0.50 and 0.03 / 0.93. The final rule (the same policy on a larger slice: validation rows plus out-of-family predictions for the team-written training rows, pooled over 10 fold assignments and weighted like the training data) is stable (suspicious 0.34 on two disjoint halves) but still reflects team-written wording. Independent scams score lower than that slice does, so the chosen cut caught fewer of them than the slice promised (slice recall 0.974, probe recall 0.945).
- **Likely cause of the drop from round 1 to final (not verified by an ablation, the scoring budget did not allow one):** awareness tips and scams share vocabulary (OTP, PIN, arrest, "never share") and word and character n-grams cannot see negation, so every awareness structure added in round 2 lowered the scores of real scams that use those words. One repository test showed it directly: after round 2 a Hindi "scan this QR and enter your PIN to receive money" scam fell below the suspicious cut, because the new awareness rows said "never scan a QR code to receive money". Scam variants of the same shapes were added as the counterpart, which fixed the test but does not remove the tension.

## What to try next

1. Choose the cuts on a set of independently written messages (for example 150 written by a third person and never used for anything else) instead of team-written slices. This is the cheapest fix for the operating-point problem.
2. Get a fresh scoring budget and re-measure the round-1 data state with the cuts chosen that way; it came closest (it failed only the probe false-flag count and the evasion count by 2).
3. Do not add more awareness rows; give the model something that sees negation ("never share", "do not scan"), for example one more signal, or fewer but more varied awareness examples with a low weight.
4. Re-base the limits for items 2, 4 and 5 on a clean baseline: the leak-free numbers of `2026-11-23.3` are not known, and the flattered ones are a hard target for a clean model.

## How to read this

- The **test** split is group-aware (no template shared with training) but most of its text comes from public datasets that look templated and mostly English, so its scores are **optimistic**. The challenge set and the blind, probe, OTP and clip sets (written by other people, never trained on) are the more honest numbers.
- The **challenge** set is hand-written and independent of the training data, but was inspected during development, so it is not a fully blind test. Counts are small, so every percentage carries a wide interval (shown above).
- The generated hard-negative families were written in earlier sessions that had seen failures on some of these evaluation sets; the leak guard removes exact and near copies, but the probe and blind results may still be somewhat flattering.
- Hindi (Devanagari) training data is small (a few hundred team-written rows); expect weaker results there.
- No on-phone timing is reported here; see the Vitest parity/timing test for TypeScript scoring speed.
